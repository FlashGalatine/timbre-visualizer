# Ink: coloured ink poured into a tank of water — plan

**Goal:** Ink becomes a scene on the scene kit: soft specks of ink poured into a floating
glass tank, one place and colour for each of the eight lowest bands.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-21-ink-tank-design.md`

**Architecture:** `overlay/ink.js` assigns `globalThis.TimbreInk`: the arithmetic, then the
shaders, then `definition()` for `TimbreScene.factory`. `overlay/factories/ink.js` is one
line. Checks first: a section of `verify.mjs` for the arithmetic and the wiring, a block of
`verify-render.mjs` for the picture.

## Constraints

- Classic `<script>`, no build; the file must also load under Node and touch nothing at
  load (no `document`, `THREE`, `TimbreScene` or core.js globals).
- No `Math.random`: a seeded sequence.
- All timing from `dt`; the ink moves in steps no longer than a sixtieth of a second.
- The effect id `ink` and its `swirl` setting stay, so saved configs keep working.

## The arithmetic (`TimbreInk`)

| Function | What it gives |
|---|---|
| `speckCount(density)` | specks at once: 6,000 at 0, 30,000 at 100 |
| `shareOf(count)` | the count as a share of the most |
| `loudness(bins, band, gain)`, `idle(b, t)`, `settle(v)` | what a band hears |
| `placeOf(out, b, clock)` | where band `b` pours: across and front to back |
| `pourRate(level, share)` | specks a second from a band at a level |
| `strengthOf(flame, swirl)` | how strong the currents are |
| `current(out, x, y, z, t, strength)` | the water's current at a place and time |
| `nearGlass(out, x, y, z)` | how much of the current is left near the glass, floor and surface |
| `twistOf(whirl, n)`, `whirlAfter(whirl, dt)` | a beat's twist, and how it dies away |
| `stepsFor(dt)` | how many steps a frame takes |
| `createInk(n, seed)`, `pour(ink, count, b, level, clock, colour)` | the specks, and one more poured |
| `stepInk(ink, count, h, clock, strength, whirl)` | a step of every speck; returns how many are left |
| `lookOf(out, age, size, share)`, `litOf(y)` | a speck's size, how much it covers, how lit it is |
| `glassOf(glass, flame, kick)`, `surfaceOf(glass, flame, kick)` | how bright the glass and the surface are |
| `measure(ink, count)` | for the checks: how many specks each band has, where, and the box they lie in |

## Tasks

1. Checks of the arithmetic in `verify.mjs` (they fail), then the arithmetic.
2. The scene, the factory, the table (`VIZ_SCENES`, defaults for `swirl`, `density`,
   `glass`, `cameraDrift`), the three pages' script tags.
3. The picture checks in `verify-render.mjs`, run and measured. The fresh-page check that
   uses Ink as "an older WebGL effect" moves to Shards.
4. `docs/EFFECTS.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`.
5. One independent review; its fixes, each with a check first.
6. Deliberate breaks of every line; a check for each that nothing caught.
7. Both suites, final stills, the owner's look in OBS.

## Review focus

- A speck older than its life (the spike drew square holes there).
- Ink that leaves the tank, or piles into its corners.
- A page that is small: specks sized for the kit's larger picture.
- The mic muted while ink is in the water: it thins away and the picture comes to rest.
- Density changed while ink is in the water.

## Found during the build

- **The spike's square holes.** A speck a frame older than its life had the square root of a
  negative number for its cover. A speck that is gone, or too thin to see, is nothing.
- **Specks read as dots** until each was made thickest in its middle, thinning smoothly to
  nothing at its rim, and wide enough to lie over its neighbours.
- **A tenth of the ink lay in a heap on the floor.** The current only died away at the
  glass and the floor, so what reached them stayed. They turn ink back now.
- **The Glass setting** was asked for by the owner while this was being built.

## What the review found

One independent review (it ran the arithmetic in long simulations, loaded three.js under
Node for the geometry and the camera, and replayed the checks' number-reading parts; no
suite, no browser). Each of these was fixed with a check first:

- **With music, ink was poured over while it was still in the water.** More was poured in a
  speck's lifetime than there are specks, so every speck was cut off at about four seconds
  of its eight and a half, when it showed most. The pour is held to what the specks can
  hold (`pourShare`).
- **At a high Swirl a third of the ink gathered in the tank's upright edges.** The check for
  it had passed by the luck of when its beats fell. The glass turns ink back harder the
  stronger the current, and the check runs seven cases.
- **Ink hid the tank's near edges.** The whole tank was drawn before the ink. Its far faces
  are drawn before the ink now, its near faces and the surface after.
- **See-through ink in the bands where the kit dims the picture's edges was darkened but
  still covered.** The frame is taller and lower, so the tank stands clear of them.
- **A beat's ring was mirrored front to back**, and a check written before the review came
  back had expected it so.
- **The rule that turned ink outward along the floor never fired**: ink was never fast
  enough when it got there. It was taken out, and the documents no longer say the ink
  spreads along the floor.
- **Cost.** Nine sines for every speck, every step, was most of it. The current is read
  from tables made once a step; specks too thin to see are not drawn; only the specks in
  use are sent to be drawn. A full tank of 30,000: 12.3 ms a frame before, 4.7 after, with
  the GPU's share. The cost checks now fill the tank first and count the GPU's time.
- The palette is decoded as three.js decodes it, as the glass's is.
- **Checks for paths that had none**: where a ring starts and the four rings in turn; a
  second beat's twist added to the first; the twist moving the ink; where a band pours
  wandering with time; Density lowered and raised again; the empty tank's light and where
  it lies; how bright the ink is; the steps a frame takes.

Left as it is, and said in the design: the kit's last pass treats a see-through pixel as
partly light, so thin ink is brighter than a pigment would be; Swirl at 0 still lets a beat
twist the tank; and moving the Density slider makes the ink jump.

## Breaking it on purpose

Every line was broken, one at a time, in a separate copy of the branch, each against its
own section of the checks (the baseline run, with nothing broken, said that nothing
failed).

- **Arithmetic: 166 breaks of the code as it stands.** Eight were caught by nothing at
  first, seven of them before the review and one after, and each now has a check that its
  break fails: a count of specks that was not a whole number; the bands pouring together
  when idle and not in turn; a frame a fiftieth of a second long taking one step; a stream
  thicker in its middle than at its edge; a stream's edge not moving outward; a speck that
  is gone going on ageing; the camera's zoom left out of a speck's size; and the floor not
  turning ink back harder in a current.
- **The scene and the shaders: 96 breaks.** Ten were caught by nothing at first:
  - the glass's edges, and the surface's, sized for the kit's larger picture on the small
    page (the empty tank's light there is now measured);
  - the ring's light left out (the picture now counts pixels that are bright half a second
    after a beat and were not at the beat: 319 with the ring, none without);
  - a speck sized for a fixed picture and not this one (at half size the ink must cover a
    quarter of what it did);
  - the currents following the level and not the voice;
  - the ring's next place not brought back within the count when Density is lowered. This
    one showed that the check's own count of stray specks always read none: a field of
    the same name hid it;
  - every band pouring alike when idle;
  - a current that never changes with time, and ink all lit alike. For both, the scene's
    ink is now made again in the check, step for step from the arithmetic, and must be the
    same specks in the same places, drawn in the same colours;
  - and only the first third of the specks sent to be drawn (no run had poured enough to
    show it; the flooded tank's picture is now measured).
  Each new check was confirmed by running its break again.
